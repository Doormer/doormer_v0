How to take a homework photo that solves first time

A clear photo gives Doormer the same thing your teacher sees: the printed question, its diagram, and the details needed to work through it.

Updated 10 October 2026 · 5 min read

  8. Keep reading
  • Doormer can only solve from the printed question, numbers, labels and diagrams it can read.
  • Use one question per photo, with every part and reference it needs in frame.
  • Bright even light, a flat angle and sharp focus prevent most retakes.
  • If a message asks for another try, change one clear thing and upload again.

Why the photo matters

When you upload a homework photo, Doormer is not looking at the page the way a person can lean closer, guess a smudged digit, or ask what a cropped label says. It reads the printed question: the words, numbers, units, labels, and any diagram, table, graph or passage. It sets aside your own writing, including crossings-out, circled answers, rough working and rewritten values.

That is helpful when your page is messy, because a few abandoned attempts should not confuse the solution. But it also means the printed information has to be visible. If a triangle side is covered by your hand, if a graph axis is cropped, or if glare hides the units, Doormer cannot use that missing detail.

Think like the solver

Before you take the photo, ask: could someone solve this if this image was all they had? If the answer is no, include the missing part before you upload.

Include the whole question

Use one question per photo. If your worksheet has questions 3, 4 and 5 in a row, photograph only the one you want solved. If question 4 has parts a, b and c, include the parts you need. Do not cut off part a if part b says "using your answer above".

Include every diagram, table, graph, map or short reading passage that belongs to the question. Include units such as metres, grams or seconds. Include labels on axes and shapes, even if they sit near the edge of the page. Notes such as "not to scale" matter too, because they tell you not to trust how long a side looks in the picture.

If the question refers to an earlier part or a value from a worked example, put that earlier information in the frame as well. For example, "Use your answer to part ii" cannot be solved from part iii alone unless part ii and its printed information are visible.

Light, angle and focus

Good light is the simplest upgrade. Put the page near a bright window or under an even room light. Move your phone or hand so it does not cast a shadow over the question. If the paper is glossy, tilt the page slightly until the glare moves away from the printed text.

Hold the phone flat above the page, not from a steep side angle. A side angle turns rectangles into slants and can make graphs or tables harder to read. If you are using a thick book, press the page gently so the curve near the spine flattens. You do not need the page to look neat; you need the printed details to be clear.

Fill most of the frame with the question, but leave a small margin around it so labels are not clipped. Tap the question on your screen to focus. Hold still for a moment after tapping the shutter, especially in dim light. Blurry numbers are one of the fastest ways to turn a good question into a retake.

Messy pages are fine. Your own working can be crossed out, circled or half-rubbed away. Just make sure your writing does not cover printed numbers, labels, units or important words. If it does, use a clean copy, move a sticky note, or take the photo from a spot where the printed question is visible.

Screens and file types

If the question is on a laptop or tablet screen, a screenshot is usually better than photographing the screen. Screens can add stripes, glare and blur, while a screenshot keeps the text sharp. Crop the screenshot so it contains one question and the details it needs.

Doormer accepts photos from your camera or gallery as JPG, PNG or HEIC. Many iPhones save photos as HEIC by default, and that is fine. You do not need to convert the file before uploading.

What upload messages mean

If you see "We could not read it", Doormer is saying the photo is too blurry, dark or cropped to solve safely. Retake it with brighter light, the whole question in frame, and a steadier hand. It asks for a retake rather than guessing, which is the right response when a number or label cannot be read.

If you see "This does not look like a question", check that the image contains an actual homework question, not just notes, a blank page or your working. If the question is on another part of the page, crop or retake the photo so the question is clear.

If you see "Solver timed out", the solve took too long. Keep the page open while Doormer works; solving can take from a few seconds to a few minutes. If it times out, try again with the clearest version of the question.

Pre-snap checklist

  • One question is in the photo, not a whole page of unrelated questions.
  • All parts you need are visible, including earlier parts the question mentions.
  • The diagram, table, graph or passage is included.
  • Units, labels, axis names and "not to scale" notes are not cropped.
  • The page is in bright, even light with no hand or phone shadow.
  • The phone is held flat above the page, and curved book pages are flattened.
  • The question fills the frame with a small margin around it.
  • You tapped to focus and held the phone steady.
  • Your own writing does not cover printed numbers or labels.

How Doormer helps

Doormer runs in a web browser on a phone, tablet or computer, so there is no download. You can upload a photo from the camera or gallery. It works best with school homework questions, especially maths and science, where working and diagrams matter.

Once the image is readable, the solution starts with "The plan" and then moves one step at a time. Each step has a "Why this works" note. When a picture helps, Doormer draws a diagram from the printed numbers in the question and checks it against the words beside it. The answer sits in a vault at the end, and "Check it" shows another way to check the result, including the form and units asked for.
