Trigonometry and parallelogram area

Briefing

The plan

  1. Use the 4 m road width and the 5 m gap on the left side to find how steeply the road slopes.
  2. Use that slope and the 24 m drop to find the width of the field.
  3. The road’s part of the field is a parallelogram — find its area.

  • Road width4 m
  • Left side, from the top71 m · 5 m · 24 m

Your working on the page was set aside.

Level 1 of 3

Find the road’s slope

Call θ the angle each road edge makes below the horizontal. On the left side, the 5 m gap between the road edges, the 4 m road width and a third side form a right-angled triangle.

52−42=25−16=3

So the road drops 3 for every 4 across:

tan⁡θ=34
Why this works

The 5 m gap is the hypotenuse: it runs straight down between the road edges, while the 4 m width is measured at right angles to the road.

Level 2 of 3

Find the field’s width

The lower road edge enters on the left side and leaves at the bottom-right corner, so it drops the printed 24 m across the field. Call the width W.

24W=34
W=24×43=32m

The 71 m label marks where the upper edge enters; this step doesn’t need it.

Why this works

The lower edge drops 24 m, not 71 m or 100 m, because it starts at the lower road edge on the left side and ends at the bottom-right corner.

Level 3 of 3

Work out the area

Inside the field, the road is a parallelogram. Its two vertical sides are 5 m long and 32 m apart.

A=5×32=160m2
Why this works

A parallelogram’s area is base × perpendicular height. Take a 5 m vertical side as the base; the perpendicular height is the field’s width, 32 m.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Something else on your mind? Get in touch.

Is Doormer free?

Yes. Doormer is free to use, with no subscription and no payment details. Solving each question takes a powerful AI, so while a photo is being solved you may see one ad. That’s how Doormer stays free for students.

What can I ask it?

Homework questions from school subjects — especially maths and science, where working and diagrams matter most. Snap one question at a time, with any diagram, table or graph it needs.

Is using Doormer cheating?

For homework practice, no. You see every step and why it works before the answer. For assessed work, follow your school’s rules and ask your teacher if you’re unsure.

How is it different from ChatGPT?

Chatbots are built for conversation. Doormer is built for homework photos: a plan, steps with diagrams, a check, and the answer last.

Do I need to download anything?

No. Doormer runs in the web browser on your phone, tablet or computer. On a phone, you can take the photo straight from your camera.

What makes a good photo?

Bright light, a flat page, and the whole question in the frame, including labels and diagrams. Messy working on the page is fine — Doormer sets it aside. JPG, PNG and HEIC all work. Our photo guide has more tips.

How long does solving take?

From a few seconds to a few minutes, depending on the question. Keep the page open — your solution opens by itself as soon as it’s ready.

Is every answer correct?

No AI is right every time. Doormer checks its work and shows you the check, but for anything important, compare with your class notes or ask your teacher.

What are quarks and cards?

Quarks are points you earn by finishing questions. Spend them on card draws to build your collection, starting with Meridian — more collections are on the way. Quarks and cards are just for fun and have no cash value.

Can I see my old questions?

Yes. Every question you solve is kept in Saved, so you can come back to the steps before a test.
